# Ophthalmology-3D-parabolic-surface-of-the-human-cornea
This script models the 3D parabolic surface of the human cornea. It compares a normal, rotationally symmetric cornea (circular paraboloid) with an astigmatic cornea (elliptical paraboloid with different curvatures).
The Biconic Surface Model: In ophthalmology, the shape of the cornea is often approximated using biconic or parabolic surfaces. The mathematical equation used here to determine the elevation ($Z$) of the cornea is:$$Z = \frac{X^2}{2R_x} + \frac{Y^2}{2R_y}$$Where $R_x$ and $R_y$ are the radii of curvature along the orthogonal meridians.Normal Cornea (Z_normal): When the eye is perfectly shaped (no astigmatism), the curvature is the same in all directions ($R_x = R_y$). This creates a perfectly symmetric circular paraboloid, meaning light rays entering the eye will converge at a single focal point on the retina.Astigmatic Cornea (Z_astigmatic): In an eye with astigmatism, the cornea is shaped more like an American football than a perfectly round basketball. This is represented mathematically by making $R_x \neq R_y$. This creates an elliptical paraboloid.Optical Consequence: Because of the asymmetric parabolic shape in the second plot, light traveling through the vertical axis will focus at a different distance than light traveling through the horizontal axis, leading to blurred vision. Optometrists use these exact surface equations to calculate the corrective cylindrical lenses needed for glasses.
